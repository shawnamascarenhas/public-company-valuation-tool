# Recognizable-Company Case Study: Apple

## Purpose and valuation date

This case study demonstrates how the platform's analytical framework can be
communicated using a recognizable listed company. It is a compact public-data
exercise, not the audited fictional model packaged with the application and
not an investment recommendation.

- Target: Apple Inc. (`AAPL`)
- Financial period: fiscal year ended September 27, 2025
- Market snapshot: September 24, 2026
- Currency: USD

## Historical operating picture

Apple reported the following fiscal-year results in its 2025 Form 10-K:

| USD millions | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 383,285 | 391,035 | 416,161 |
| Operating income | 114,301 | 123,216 | 133,050 |
| Depreciation and amortization | 11,519 | 11,445 | 11,698 |

Sources: [Apple 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320193/000032019325000079/aapl-20250927.htm), [Apple Investor Relations](https://investor.apple.com/investor-relations/).

The figures imply:

- approximately 4.2% revenue CAGR between fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2025;
- operating-margin expansion from 29.8% to 32.0%;
- 2025 Services revenue of $109.2 billion, or 26.2% of total revenue; and
- 14% year-over-year Services growth in fiscal 2025.

The central operating story is therefore not rapid consolidated growth. It is
modest revenue expansion combined with high profitability and a growing,
higher-margin Services mix.

## Illustrative relative-valuation snapshot

The dated market snapshot used the following observed P/E ratios:

| Company | Ticker | P/E |
|---|---|---:|
| Apple | AAPL | 38.69x |
| Microsoft | MSFT | 29.80x |
| Alphabet | GOOGL | 16.97x |
| Oracle | ORCL | 22.66x |

Reference quote pages: [Apple](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/aapl), [Microsoft](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/msft), [Alphabet](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/googl), and [Oracle](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/orcl).

The three-company peer median is 22.66x. Apple therefore traded at an
approximately 70.8% premium to that median in the snapshot. Applying the peer
median mechanically to Apple's $8.71 snapshot EPS would imply approximately
$197 per share versus the observed $337 share price.

This is intentionally presented as a diagnostic, not a price target. Microsoft,
Alphabet, and Oracle differ materially from Apple in product mix, recurring
revenue, capital intensity, growth, and balance-sheet structure. A professional
analysis would expand and segment the peer set, normalize earnings, and combine
the result with a fully reviewed DCF.

## Plain-English conclusion

Apple's underlying financial profile remained strong: revenue returned to
healthier growth, operating margin expanded, and Services increased its share
of the business. The market snapshot, however, priced Apple at a substantial
P/E premium to the selected large-cap technology references.

That premium may reflect brand strength, ecosystem durability, capital returns,
and expectations for future products and Services growth. It also means the
valuation leaves less room for execution disappointment. On this limited
relative basis, the shares appear expensive rather than obviously undervalued.
A defensible investment conclusion would require the complete model, including
a broader peer review and a DCF with explicit growth, margin, reinvestment, WACC,
and terminal assumptions.

## How to discuss this in an interview

1. Start with the operating trend: 4.2% two-year revenue CAGR and margin
   expansion to roughly 32%.
2. Explain the mix shift: Services reached approximately 26% of revenue and
   grew faster than the company overall.
3. Separate quality from price: a strong business can still screen as expensive.
4. Acknowledge peer limitations before presenting the implied value.
5. Explain that the platform makes assumptions and source provenance visible so
   the analyst can challenge the result instead of accepting an automated answer.
